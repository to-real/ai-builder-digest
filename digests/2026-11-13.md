## Dan Shipper

*CEO, Every*

**Your perspective is the alpha in a world where the default AI answer is free.**

Shipper ran Every's customer survey and gave the same data + same AI to his whole team. Each person found completely different insights: he spotted a teams-selling opportunity, Yash found a churn problem from too much email, Douglas discovered readers feel lonely, Jack built a Claymation game show. The default Claude prompt produces generic truths everyone already has. His evals lead ran an experiment: Claude can predict his Slack messages 93% of the time if it gets 400 guesses, but can only rank his actual answer in the top 10% about 60% of the time. "Generation is really cheap, but ranking is hard." That's why teams matter: each person's responsibility narrows where they look, and their perspective ranks what matters. As models improve, they raise the floor for everyone, so the default answer becomes baseline. The value is pushing past it with your perspective. He also argues we should flip how we measure AI: not consensus benchmarks, but "how good is it at doing my work, from my perspective?"

Shipper 用 Every 的客户调查做实验：同样的数据 + 同样的 AI 给全团队，每个人挖出截然不同的洞察——他看到团队销售机会，Yash 发现邮件过多导致流失，Douglas 发现读者感到孤独，Jack 做了个黏土动画游戏节目。默认的 Claude prompt 产出的是人人都有的泛泛之谈。他的 evals 负责人做了实验：Claude 给 400 次机会时 93% 能猜中他的 Slack 消息，但让它自己排序时，他的真实回答只有约 60% 的概率进入前 10%。"生成很便宜，但排序很难。"这就是团队的价值所在：每个人的职责缩小搜索范围，视角决定什么值得行动。模型越强，地板越高，默认答案就越不值钱，超额收益来自你用视角突破基线。他还认为衡量 AI 的方式应该翻转：不是共识基准测试，而是"从我的视角看，它做我的工作有多好"。

## Aditya Agarwal

*General Partner, SPC*

**The vast majority of model-driven returns will come from the frontier, not the diffusion.**

Agarwal maps two company categories for the AI era. Category 1, "Diffusion of AI," is today's vertical SaaS equivalent: get AI close to where work happens (Profound, Cognition, Harvey). Category 2, "Frontier of AI," uses AI to accelerate scientific progress, then builds products from that progress. He lists SPC portfolio examples: Externa adds new chemical building blocks to proteins for precision drug design, Epana Bio designs autoimmune therapies from genomics, VenomicsAI does venom-based drug discovery, Equivariant does multi-physics modeling for semiconductors. "Category 2 is not well understood or much talked about externally. It should be because I think that is where the vast majority of model driven returns will come from."

Agarwal 画出 AI 时代两类公司的地图。第一类"AI 扩散"是今天的垂直 SaaS 等价物：让 AI 贴近工作现场（Profound、Cognition、Harvey）。第二类"AI 前沿"用 AI 加速科学进步，再从进步中构建产品。他列举 SPC 投资组合：Externa 给蛋白质添加新化学构建模块以实现精准药物设计，Epana Bio 用基因组学设计自免疫疗法，VenomicsAI 做基于毒液的药物发现，Equivariant 做半导体多物理场建模。"第二类外界理解不多、讨论不多。但应该被讨论，因为我认为绝大多数模型驱动的回报将来自这里。"

## AI & I by Every — A New Vision for Human Work After Automation

*AI & I by Every*

**Great human work after automation is the perspective you bring to the model, not the model's default output.**

Dan Shipper, Every's co-founder and CEO, closes their Thesis 2027 conference with a framework that reframes the AI-job anxiety narrative. He distinguishes two ways to predict the future: the aggregate (extrapolate from big data, like OpenAI's GDPval benchmark showing AI beating humans 83% of the time) and the intimate (zoom into one real case and extrapolate from the messiness). The aggregate makes you think humans are doomed. The intimate reveals something else. Shipper describes doing the work of five people in one week with AI: data analysis, game development, CRM building, outreach. But when his whole team used the same survey and same AI, each person surfaced completely different insights based on their responsibility and perspective. The default model answer is becoming commoditized baseline. The alpha is your non-consensus perspective. He introduces the "allocation economy": you'll be judged not on what you know, but on how well you allocate intelligence to get work done. And he proposes flipping how we measure AI: from consensus benchmarks to "how good is it at doing my work, from my perspective?" As he puts it: "Generation is really cheap, but ranking is hard. And that should tell you something really important about why teams are valuable in AI and ultimately why humans are valuable in AI."

Dan Shipper，Every 联合创始人兼 CEO，在 Thesis 2027 大会闭幕演讲中提出了一个重新框定 AI 就业焦虑的框架。他区分两种预测未来的方式：聚合式（从大数据外推，如 OpenAI 的 GDPval 基准显示 AI 83% 的时间胜过人类）和亲密式（放大一个真实案例，从复杂性中外推）。聚合式让你觉得人类完了。亲密式揭示了别的可能。Shipper 描述自己一周内用 AI 完成五个人的工作：数据分析、游戏开发、CRM 搭建、客户触达。但当全团队用同样的调查和同样的 AI 时，每个人基于各自的职责和视角挖出了完全不同的洞察。默认的模型输出正在变成商品化基线，超额收益来自你的非共识视角。他提出"分配经济"：你将被评判的不是你知道多少，而是你多善于分配智能来完成工作。他还建议翻转衡量 AI 的方式：从共识基准测试转向"从我的视角看，它做我的工作有多好"。正如他所说："生成很便宜，但排序很难。这告诉你一些真正重要的东西，关于为什么团队在 AI 中有价值，以及归根结底为什么人类在 AI 中有价值。"
