## Latent Space — Controlling Biology: Fabian Theis, Helmholtz Munich

*Latent Space*

**Virtual cells are moving from mapping to prediction to steering — and Lab Compass is the tool that closes the loop.**

Fabian Theis leads the Computational Health Center at Helmholtz Munich (~500-600 people, Europe's largest academic health AI group). His lab created Scanpy and the scVerse ecosystem, tools most computational biologists use daily. The core arc: we built cell atlases (mapping), then perturbation prediction models (prediction), and now we can actually steer the next experiment. His new system Lab Compass is an inverse generative model — given a forward world model and a desired cell state, it tells you what perturbation to apply to get there. They ran 3-4 lab-in-the-loop cycles with collaborators at Cambridge on hematopoiesis, iteratively improving the model.

The data landscape is exploding: Pars Bio measured 10 million transcriptomes in a single experiment, and perturbation spaces (CRISPR knockouts, drug batteries) are scaling from sparse controls-vs-disease to rich high-dimensional screens. Theis's scConcert model now covers 350M cells across 16 species at 100M parameters, showing real scaling behavior. But he's honest about where foundation models fell short: early virtual cell papers oversold complexity when linear baselines often matched performance. The Arc Institute's Virtual Cell Challenge was a "glass of cold water" — though the true winner (flow matching-based CellFlow, now accepted to Nature Methods) did need nonlinearity. The key lesson: "I always tell my students, please, you know, have the baseline."

On commercialization: platform-only virtual cell companies keep pivoting to "we make a drug" because investors demand it. The moat, Theis argues, is coupling models with unique data generation — pharma buys into consortia like the Cancer Organoid Perturbation Atlas for early data access. His Y-model PhD program requires two advisors (methods + wet lab), forcing computational students to deeply understand biological problems before modeling them. The ultimate vision: "We want to RL everything. We want to do RL also in this loop. It's a bit out there, but I think we're slowly getting there."

虚拟细胞正在从建图走向预测再走向操控——Lab Compass 是闭环的关键工具。

Fabian Theis 领导 Helmholtz Munich 的计算健康中心（约 500-600 人，欧洲最大的学术健康 AI 团队）。他的实验室创建了 Scanpy 和 scVerse 生态，大多数计算生物学家每天都在用。核心脉络：先建细胞图谱（建图），再做扰动预测模型（预测），现在可以真正操控下一个实验。他的新系统 Lab Compass 是一个逆生成模型——给定前向世界模型和目标细胞状态，告诉你施加什么扰动才能到达那里。他们和剑桥合作者在造血系统上跑了 3-4 轮 lab-in-the-loop，迭代改进模型。

数据规模正在爆发：Pars Bio 单次实验测了 1000 万转录组，扰动空间从稀疏的对照-疾病对扩展到丰富的高维筛选。scConcert 模型已覆盖 16 个物种的 3.5 亿细胞、1 亿参数，展现出真实的 scaling 行为。但他对 foundation model 的局限很坦诚：早期虚拟细胞论文过度推销复杂度，线性基线常常就够了。Arc Institute 的虚拟细胞挑战赛是一杯"冷水"——不过真正的赢家（基于 flow matching 的 CellFlow，已被 Nature Methods 接收）确实需要非线性。核心教训："我总告诉学生，请把基线做好。"

商业化方面：纯平台型虚拟细胞公司不断转向"我们做药"，因为投资人要求这样。Theis 认为护城河在于模型+独特数据生成的组合——药企通过 Cancer Organoid Perturbation Atlas 等联盟获取早期数据访问权。他的 Y 型博士项目要求两位导师（方法+湿实验），迫使计算方向的学生在建模前深入理解生物问题。终极愿景："我们想把一切 RL 化。在这个循环里也做 RL。有点超前，但我们在慢慢靠近。"
