*No Priors*

**The AI market is experiencing a punctuated equilibrium where trillion-dollar valuations are rare, and founders must balance ambition with the reality of accelerated timelines and potential regulatory capture.**

Sarah and Elad dissect the current investment landscape, arguing that while we've seen a historic inflection with companies like OpenAI and SpaceX reaching trillion-dollar valuations in five years, this is an anomaly rather than a new normal. They caution against assuming a flood of new trillion-dollar companies will emerge in the next 3-5 years, noting that physical constraints like compute and energy create an oligopolistic market structure. Elad introduces the concept of "return on invested tokens," suggesting that as compute becomes the scarce resource, labs will ration it based on researcher productivity, potentially leading to a burnout cycle among those fearing Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI). The conversation pivots to regulatory capture, drawing parallels to the nuclear industry where safety lobbies killed abundant clean energy; they warn that an overemphasis on AI safety without considering benefits could stifle innovation. Ultimately, they advise founders to have rational, pre-scheduled board discussions about exits, recognizing that in "AI time," three years equals a decade of normal progress.

Sarah 和 Elad 解构了当前的投资格局，认为虽然我们见证了 OpenAI 和 SpaceX 等公司在五年内达到万亿美元估值的历史性拐点，但这是一种异常现象而非新常态。他们警告不要假设在未来 3-5 年内会有大量新的万亿美元公司涌现，指出计算和能源等物理限制创造了一个寡头垄断的市场结构。Elad 提出了“投资 Token 回报率”的概念，暗示随着计算成为稀缺资源，实验室将根据研究人员的生产力进行配给，这可能导致那些担心递归自我改进（RSI）的研究人员出现职业倦怠周期。对话转向监管俘获，将其与核工业进行类比，指出安全游说团体扼杀了丰富的清洁能源；他们警告，过分强调 AI 安全而不考虑利益可能会扼杀创新。最终，他们建议创始人进行理性的、预先安排的董事会退出讨论，认识到在“AI 时间”里，三年等于正常时间的十年。
