## AI & I by Every — How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time

*Every Podcast*

**Dots are the first AI product that has given Sam Altman back control of his mornings — and that personal-agency unlock is more revealing than any model benchmark.**

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman describes a concrete before-and-after: he used to lose his deep-work morning window because overnight emergencies forced him to check his phone, polluting his mental space. Now his Dot triages urgency, handles what it can, and surfaces only what actually needs Altman's attention. During DevDay, Tibo's Dot flagged a demo-breaking bug five minutes before the keynote and offered to fix it. Altman's Dot caught a critical item he never would have found in his back-to-back schedule. The Dot even persisted overnight to find a screenshot in Slack that Altman had given up searching for — reading every image because it knew the target was visual, not textual.

Altman frames the AI moment as a Renaissance, not an Industrial Revolution. The Renaissance was centered on people reconnecting with human potential; the Industrial Revolution turned people into cogs. He pushes back hard on the "house cats" doom narrative: "I get sad to hear people talk about how AI is going to automate everything and there's going to be no role for humans. That's certainly not what we think." The bottleneck now isn't capability — it's creative spirit. There are more buildable ideas than people to imagine them.

On Space, Altman introduces "live documents" — docs that auto-update as context changes and where multiple people's Dots collaborate to produce emergent insights. "Seven people's Dots can, working together, create something new that kind of existed in all of our heads to some degree, but was like an emergent thing." On speed, he references Bret Victor's principle that creators need immediate connection to what they're creating: ultrafast inference doesn't just save time, it fundamentally changes the iterative thinking loop.

Dots 是第一个让 Sam Altman 重新掌控早晨的 AI 产品——这个个人能动性的解锁比任何模型基准测试都更有启示性。Altman 描述了具体的前后对比：过去夜间紧急事件迫使他一早查手机，污染了深度工作的心理空间。现在他的 Dot 分诊紧急程度，处理能处理的，只把真正需要他关注的事推上来。DevDay 上，Tibo 的 Dot 在主题演讲前五分钟发现了演示破坏性 bug 并主动提出修复。Altman 的 Dot 在他连轴转的日程中抓住了一件他根本找不到的关键事项。Dot 甚至通宵坚持在 Slack 里找一张 Altman 已经放弃搜索的截图——它读了每张图片，因为它知道目标是视觉内容而非文本。

Altman 把当前 AI 时刻定义为文艺复兴而非工业革命。文艺复兴以人重新连接人类潜能为中心；工业革命把人变成齿轮。他强烈反对"家猫"末日叙事："听到人们说 AI 会自动化一切、人类没有角色，我很难过。这当然不是我们的想法。"现在的瓶颈不是能力，而是创造精神。可构建的想法比能想象它们的人多。

在 Space 方面，Altman 引入"活文档"——随上下文变化自动更新、多个人的 Dot 协作产生涌现洞察的文档。"七个人的 Dot 一起工作，能创造出某种程度存在于我们所有人脑海中、但又是涌现出来的新东西。"在速度方面，他引用 Bret Victor 的原则：创作者需要与所创之物即时连接——ultrafast 推理不仅节省时间，它根本性地改变了迭代思维循环。
