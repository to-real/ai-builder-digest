## Training Data — Google's AI Infrastructure Chief, Amin Vahdat, on the Physics & Economics of Frontier AI

*Training Data*

**FLOPS is a vanity metric. At frontier scale, what matters is "goodput" — the actual useful computation delivered after failures, recovery, and restarts eat your throughput.**

Amin Vahdat, Head of Google's AI Infrastructure, oversees one of the largest capital buildouts in history (Google alone: $200B+ CapEx this year). His core insight is that raw FLOPS tell you almost nothing. At 100,000-accelerator scale, something fails multiple times per hour, and each failure in a synchronous workload can halt the entire job. The time spent detecting, checkpointing, and restarting is work that produces zero output — that's the gap between throughput and goodput. Google holds itself accountable to delivered goodput per watt, not theoretical chip benchmarks.

AI data centers are fundamentally different from traditional ones: they're purpose-built and co-designed with hardware down to cooling and power distribution. A storage rack draws 10-40 kW; an AI rack hits hundreds of kW today and may reach megawatts. You can't make a building fungible across that range without massive overbuilding. This specialization is why Google split TPU into 8I (inference) and 8T (training) chips this year — inference demand is large enough to justify a dedicated chip that's 2x faster for serving.

The DeepMind-hardware collaboration is uniquely deep: teams work shoulder-to-shoulder, sometimes delaying tape-outs by weeks to accommodate model optimizations. "We're using Gemini to design hardware for future Geminis as well." Most gains in intelligence per watt come from model-side improvements, not silicon. Google roughly doubles token-generation capacity every six months, and as much of that comes from software as from hardware.

Power is the single most fundamental long-term constraint. Google prefers grid connection with years of co-planning with utilities over behind-the-meter generation, leveraging statistical multiplexing for reliability. Orbital data centers are a real moonshot: 1.4x solar capacity (no atmospheric attenuation), 90-100% sunlight coverage vs. 28-30% on land, carbon-free, and no batteries — but cooling is harder and repairs are harder.

As Vahdat put it: "The stunning thing is that the TPU architecture at a medium level of detail hasn't really changed since TPU v1." The primitives — large matrix multiply units, sparse cores, remote load/store — have endured across a decade of model evolution. That's why betting on specialization was contrarian in 2013 but obviously right in 2026.

Amin Vahdat 掌管 Google AI 基础设施，监督人类历史上最大规模资本建设之一（仅 Google 今年 CapEx 超 2000 亿美元）。核心洞察：原始 FLOPS 几乎没有意义。在 10 万加速器规模下，每小时故障多次，同步工作流中一次故障就能让整个任务停摆。检测、检查点、重启所花的时间产出为零——这就是吞吐量与 goodput 之间的差距。Google 用实际交付的 goodput per watt 而非理论芯片基准来考核。

AI 数据中心与传统数据中心根本不同：它们是专用建筑，与硬件协同设计到冷却和配电层面。存储机架耗电 10-40 kW，AI 机架已达数百 kW，未来可能达兆瓦。在这个范围内做通用设计必然过度建设。这就是 Google 今年将 TPU 拆分为 8I（推理）和 8T（训练）的原因——推理需求大到值得专门做一块快 2 倍的芯片。

DeepMind 与硬件团队的合作深度罕见：肩并肩工作，有时为配合模型优化延迟流片数周。"我们正在用 Gemini 为未来的 Gemini 设计硬件。" 智力每瓦特的大部分增益来自模型侧而非硅片。Google 大约每六个月将 token 生成能力翻倍，软件贡献与硬件相当。

电力是最根本的长期约束。Google 偏好电网连接并提前数年与电力公司联合规划，利用统计复用获得可靠性。轨道数据中心是真正的登月计划：1.4 倍太阳能容量，90-100% 日照覆盖 vs 地面 28-30%，零碳，无需电池——但冷却和维修更难。

正如 Vahdat 所说："令人震惊的是，TPU 架构在中等粒度上自 TPU v1 以来就没真正变过。" 大矩阵乘法单元、稀疏核心、远程存取这些原语经历了一整代模型演进依然适用。这就是为什么 2013 年押注专用化是反共识的，而 2026 年显然正确。
