## Training Data — Making Cities Awesome: Peregrine's Nick Noone & Ben Rudolph

*Training Data*

**True innovation in public safety requires "forward deployed engineering"—deeply embedding with customers to own their outcomes—combined with an "anti-network effects" data model that prioritizes privacy and local sovereignty over centralized surveillance.**

Nick Noone and Ben Rudolph, founders of Peregrine, argue that making cities safe requires a "forward deployed engineering" mindset where you psychologically own the customer's problem rather than just shipping code. Unlike traditional data companies that build business models on hoarding information, Peregrine operates on an "anti-network effects" model: they join existing data sources without taking ownership, preserving privacy and local sovereignty. They share stories of being rejected by two dozen police departments before San Pablo PD took a chance on them, and how their AI agents now handle tasks ranging from analyzing 300GB of cold case files to identifying patterns in water rescue emergencies. Their philosophy is to be "quiet professionals" who empower institutions without seeking credit, and they believe technology choices like facial recognition should be guided by the community's context, not imposed by Silicon Valley. As Nick puts it, "The idea that we as an institution would assert... a technological red line without understanding the texture and context is not a boundary line that we think that we can assert on top of our customer."

Peregrine 的创始人 Nick Noone 和 Ben Rudolph 认为，让城市安全需要一种“前沿部署工程”思维，即从心理上拥有客户的问题，而不仅仅是交付代码。与建立在囤积信息基础上的传统数据公司不同，Peregrine 采用一种“反网络效应”模式：他们连接现有数据源而不获取所有权，从而保护隐私和本地主权。他们分享了在被二十多个警察局拒绝后，圣巴勃罗警察局终于给他们机会的故事，以及他们的 AI 代理现在如何处理从分析 300GB 的悬案数据到识别水上救援紧急情况模式等各种任务。他们的理念是做“安静的专业人士”，在不寻求功劳的情况下赋予机构权力，并认为像面部识别这样的技术选择应由社区的背景指导，而不是由硅谷强加。正如 Nick 所说，“作为一个机构，我们在不了解纹理和背景的情况下断言……一条技术红线，这不是我们认为可以在客户之上强加的界限。”
